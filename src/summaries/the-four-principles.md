        <p class="text-lg sm:text-xl text-[#ECE8EF] italic leading-relaxed">
          “The pure, upright faith—the religion of Ibrāhīm—is to worship Allah alone, making religion sincerely and solely His. When shirk enters worship, it is sullied and annulled in the same way that ritual impurity (<e>ḥadath</e>) annuls a state of purification.”

    <section class="space-y-6 reveal-on-scroll">
      <p class="text-lg sm:text-xl text-[#ECE8EF]">
        Once you recognize that associating partners with Allah (<e>Shirk</e>) corrupts acts of worship, invalidates righteous deeds, and condemns its perpetrator to eternal loss, it becomes evident that understanding its subtle realities is the most vital knowledge one can acquire.
      </p>
      <p class="text-lg sm:text-xl text-[#B3ADB9]">
        To safeguard the believer from this fatal snare, scholars have extracted four concise, decisive principles grounded in divine revelation that illuminate the line between genuine monotheism and polytheistic compromise.
      </p>
    </section>

    <!-- PRINCIPLE I -->
    <section class="space-y-6 reveal-on-scroll">
      <div class="space-y-2 border-b border-[#332f2b] pb-4">
        <span class="text-sm font-mono uppercase tracking-widest text-[#d6b278]">Principle I</span>
        <h2 class="text-3xl font-medium text-white sm:text-4xl">
          Affirmation of Lordship Alone Does Not Grant Entry into Islam
        </h2>
      </div>

      <p class="text-lg sm:text-xl text-[#ECE8EF]">
        The disbelievers whom the Messenger of Allah ﷺ fought readily acknowledged that Allah is the sole Creator, Provider, Giver of life, Causer of death, and Regulator of all cosmic affairs. Yet, this confession of His Lordship (<e>Rubūbīyah</e>) was not sufficient to admit them into the fold of Islam.
      </p>

      <!-- Proof Card -->
      <div class="bg-[#24211e] border-l-2 border-[#d6b278] p-6 rounded-r-lg space-y-3">
        <p class="text-lg sm:text-xl text-[#ECE8EF] italic leading-relaxed">
          “Say: ‘Who provides for you from the sky and the earth? Or who owns hearing and sight? And who brings out the living from the dead and brings out the dead from the living? And who disposes the affairs?’ They will say: ‘Allah.’ Say: ‘Will you not then fear Him?’”
        </p>
        <span class="text-sm font-mono text-[#d6b278] block">[Sūrah Yūnus 10:31]</span>
      </div>

      <p class="text-lg sm:text-xl text-[#B3ADB9]">
        Belief that God is the supreme architect and ruler of the universe is a universal instinct, but true faith (<e>Īmān</e>) demands that this recognition be translated into exclusive worship (<e>‘Ibādah</e>).
      </p>
    </section>

    <!-- PRINCIPLE II -->
    <section class="space-y-6 reveal-on-scroll">
      <div class="space-y-2 border-b border-[#332f2b] pb-4">
        <span class="text-sm font-mono uppercase tracking-widest text-[#d6b278]">Principle II</span>
        <h2 class="text-3xl font-medium text-white sm:text-4xl">
          The Reality of False Intercession and Nearness
        </h2>
      </div>

      <p class="text-lg sm:text-xl text-[#ECE8EF]">
        The polytheists did not claim that their idols or intermediaries created the universe or answered prayers independently. Rather, they justified their devotion by asserting that these created beings served as influential channels to draw them closer to God.
      </p>

      <!-- Grid Callout for Intentions -->
      <div class="grid grid-cols-1 gap-6 sm:grid-cols-2">
        <div class="bg-[#1c1a18] p-6 rounded-lg border border-[#332f2b] space-y-3">
          <span class="text-xs font-mono uppercase tracking-widest text-[#d6b278]">01. Seeking Proximity (Qurbah)</span>
          <h3 class="text-xl font-medium text-white">Drawing Near to God</h3>
          <p class="text-base text-[#B3ADB9]">
            They directed acts of devotion to intermediaries under the pretext that the spiritual standing of these beings would bridge the gap between them and the Almighty.
          </p>
        </div>

        <div class="bg-[#1c1a18] p-6 rounded-lg border border-[#332f2b] space-y-3">
          <span class="text-xs font-mono uppercase tracking-widest text-[#d6b278]">02. Seeking Intercession (Shafā‘ah)</span>
          <h3 class="text-xl font-medium text-white">Plea of Advocacy</h3>
          <p class="text-base text-[#B3ADB9]">
            They claimed not to seek final authority from these entities, but rather used them as advocates to present their requests before Allah.
          </p>
        </div>
      </div>

      <div class="bg-[#24211e] p-6 sm:p-8 rounded-lg border border-[#332f2b] space-y-4">
        <p class="text-lg text-[#ECE8EF] italic leading-relaxed">
          “They say: ‘We only worship them so that they may bring us nearer to Allah in position...’ In reality, they desire requests from Allah, but seek to access Him through created intermediaries.”
        </p>
        <span class="text-sm font-mono text-[#d6b278] block">— The Fallacy of Subdued Devotion</span>
      </div>
    </section>

    <!-- PRINCIPLE III -->
    <section class="space-y-6 reveal-on-scroll">
      <div class="space-y-2 border-b border-[#332f2b] pb-4">
        <span class="text-sm font-mono uppercase tracking-widest text-[#d6b278]">Principle III</span>
        <h2 class="text-3xl font-medium text-white sm:text-4xl">
          The Universal Condemnation of Intermediaries
        </h2>
      </div>

      <p class="text-lg sm:text-xl text-[#ECE8EF]">
        The Prophet ﷺ appeared among a society practicing vastly divergent forms of worship. Some venerated inanimate objects, while others revered exalted spiritual beings. Yet, the Prophet ﷺ made no distinction between them, condemning all forms of direct worship to creation equally.
      </p>

      <!-- Custom Numbered List of Objects of Worship -->
      <div class="bg-[#24211e] p-6 sm:p-8 rounded-lg border border-[#24211e] space-y-6">

        <div class="flex items-start gap-4">
          <span class="text-2xl sm:text-3xl font-bold font-mono text-[#d6b278]">01.</span>
          <div class="space-y-1">
            <h3 class="text-lg font-medium text-white sm:text-xl">Celestial Bodies</h3>
            <p class="text-base text-[#B3ADB9]">
              Worshippers of the sun and moon who sought blessings from cosmic forces.
            </p>
          </div>
        </div>

        <div class="flex items-start gap-4 border-t border-[#332f2b] pt-6">
          <span class="text-2xl sm:text-3xl font-bold font-mono text-[#d6b278]">02.</span>
          <div class="space-y-1">
            <h3 class="text-lg font-medium text-white sm:text-xl">Angelic Beings & Prophets</h3>
            <p class="text-base text-[#B3ADB9]">
              Those who invoked noble spirits and holy messengers due to their exalted rank before God.
            </p>
          </div>
        </div>

        <div class="flex items-start gap-4 border-t border-[#332f2b] pt-6">
          <span class="text-2xl sm:text-3xl font-bold font-mono text-[#d6b278]">03.</span>
          <div class="space-y-1">
            <h3 class="text-lg font-medium text-white sm:text-xl">Trees, Stones & Relics</h3>
            <p class="text-base text-[#B3ADB9]">
              Those who attached spiritual expectations to physical monuments, sacred trees, and inanimate objects.
            </p>
          </div>
        </div>

      </div>

      <p class="text-lg sm:text-xl text-[#B3ADB9]">
        This demonstrates that Islam does not merely forbid worshipping idols; it forbids directing any act of worship—whether prayer, sacrifice, vows, or supplication—to anything or anyone other than Allah, regardless of the intermediate's nobility or status.
      </p>
    </section>

    <!-- PRINCIPLE IV -->
    <section class="space-y-8 reveal-on-scroll">
      <div class="space-y-2 border-b border-[#332f2b] pb-4">
        <span class="text-sm font-mono uppercase tracking-widest text-[#d6b278]">Principle IV</span>
        <h2 class="text-3xl font-medium text-white sm:text-4xl">
          The Uncompromising Severity of Contemporary Shirk
        </h2>
      </div>

      <p class="text-lg sm:text-xl text-[#ECE8EF]">
        A critical comparison reveals that the compromise of monotheism in later times is often more severe than that practiced by ancient polytheists.
      </p>

      <!-- Comparison Grid: Ancient vs Modern -->
      <div class="grid grid-cols-1 gap-6 sm:grid-cols-2">

        <div class="bg-[#1c1a18] p-6 rounded-lg border border-[#332f2b] space-y-3">
          <div class="flex items-center justify-between border-b border-[#332f2b] pb-2">
            <span class="text-sm font-mono text-[#d6b278] uppercase">The Ancients</span>
            <span class="text-xs font-mono text-[#B3ADB9] bg-[#24211e] px-2 py-0.5 rounded">Conditional Shirk</span>
          </div>
          <p class="text-base text-[#B3ADB9]">
            In times of ease, the polytheists of old associated partners with Allah. However, when severe calamity or danger at sea struck them, they abandoned their intermediaries and called upon Allah with absolute sincerity.
          </p>
        </div>

        <div class="bg-[#1c1a18] p-6 rounded-lg border border-[#332f2b] space-y-3">
          <div class="flex items-center justify-between border-b border-[#332f2b] pb-2">
            <span class="text-sm font-mono text-[#d6b278] uppercase">The Later Generations</span>
            <span class="text-xs font-mono text-[#B3ADB9] bg-[#24211e] px-2 py-0.5 rounded">Continuous Shirk</span>
          </div>
          <p class="text-base text-[#B3ADB9]">
            In contrast, later transgressors commit shirk continuously—in times of calm as well as during times of dire distress, calling upon created beings even when facing life-threatening hardship.
          </p>
        </div>

      </div>

      <!-- Conclusion Summary Card -->
      <div class="bg-[#24211e] border-l-2 border-[#d6b278] p-6 sm:p-8 rounded-r-lg space-y-3">
        <h3 class="text-xl font-medium text-white">Summary of the Four Principles</h3>
        <p class="text-base sm:text-lg text-[#B3ADB9]">
          True monotheism is not merely an intellectual acknowledgment that God exists and creates; it is the absolute dedication of all worship to Him alone. By rejecting false intercession, avoiding all created intermediaries, and maintaining sincerity in both ease and hardship, the servant preserves the pure faith of Ibrāhīm.
        </p>
      </div>
    </section>
