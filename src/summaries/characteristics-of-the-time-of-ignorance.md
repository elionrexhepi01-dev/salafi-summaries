    <!-- Introductory Context -->
    <section class="space-y-6 reveal-on-scroll">
      <p class="text-lg sm:text-xl text-[#ECE8EF]">
        To fully appreciate the light of Islamic guidance, one must examine the darkness it came to illuminate. The pre-Islamic era of ignorance (<e>Jāhilīyyah</e>) was not merely a historical epoch, but a distinct set of psychological, spiritual, and social pathologies.
      </p>
      <p class="text-lg sm:text-xl text-[#B3ADB9]">
        By categorizing the mindsets and habits of those who opposed the Prophets, scholars demonstrate that true guidance is recognized not only through direct affirmation, but by exposing and repudiating its counter-traits.
      </p>
    </section>

    <!-- PRINCIPLE I -->
    <section class="space-y-6 reveal-on-scroll">
      <div class="space-y-2 border-b border-[#332f2b] pb-4">
        <span class="text-sm font-mono uppercase tracking-widest text-[#d6b278]">Principle I</span>
        <h2 class="text-3xl font-medium text-white sm:text-4xl">
          Spiritual Compromise & Sacralizing Creation
        </h2>
      </div>

      <p class="text-lg sm:text-xl text-[#ECE8EF]">
        The core spiritual disease of <e>Jāhilīyyah</e> was not a total denial of God, but the corruption of devotion through intermediaries and superstition. Rather than turning directly to the Creator, they directed acts of veneration toward created entities.
      </p>

      <!-- Proof / Quote Card -->
      <div class="bg-[#24211e] border-l-2 border-[#d6b278] p-6 rounded-r-lg space-y-3">
        <p class="text-lg sm:text-xl text-[#ECE8EF] italic leading-relaxed">
          “They associated the righteous in their supplications, claiming they merely sought their intercession before Allah.”
        </p>
        <span class="text-sm font-mono text-[#d6b278] block">— The Delusion of False Intercession</span>
      </div>

      <p class="text-lg sm:text-xl text-[#B3ADB9]">
        This mindset manifested in concrete physical rituals. They transformed the graves of Prophets and pious figures into places of worship (<e>masājid</e>) and placed lamps and illuminations around them. Beyond grave veneration, they sought natural blessings through cosmic omens—such as attributing rainfall to the movements of stars rather than the direct decree of God.
      </p>
    </section>

    <!-- PRINCIPLE II -->
    <section class="space-y-6 reveal-on-scroll">
      <div class="space-y-2 border-b border-[#332f2b] pb-4">
        <span class="text-sm font-mono uppercase tracking-widest text-[#d6b278]">Principle II</span>
        <h2 class="text-3xl font-medium text-white sm:text-4xl">
          Epistemological Fallacies & Social Disunity
        </h2>
      </div>

      <p class="text-lg sm:text-xl text-[#ECE8EF]">
        The society of <e>Jāhilīyyah</e> substituted divine revelation with flawed human metrics for determining truth. They relied on cognitive shortcuts, social biases, and tribal loyalty rather than sound evidence.
      </p>

      <!-- Grid Callout for Epistemological Fallacies -->
      <div class="grid grid-cols-1 gap-6 sm:grid-cols-2">

        <div class="bg-[#1c1a18] p-6 rounded-lg border border-[#332f2b] space-y-3">
          <span class="text-xs font-mono uppercase tracking-widest text-[#d6b278]">01. Consensus Fallacy</span>
          <h3 class="text-xl font-medium text-white">Deception by the Majority</h3>
          <p class="text-base text-[#B3ADB9]">
            They assumed that if a custom or belief was held by the majority, it served as definitive proof of its correctness.
          </p>
        </div>

        <div class="bg-[#1c1a18] p-6 rounded-lg border border-[#332f2b] space-y-3">
          <span class="text-xs font-mono uppercase tracking-widest text-[#d6b278]">02. Materialistic Bias</span>
          <h3 class="text-xl font-medium text-white">Equating Power with Truth</h3>
          <p class="text-base text-[#B3ADB9]">
            They assumed the rich and powerful were inherently guided, while dismissing the poor and weak as followers of falsehood.
          </p>
        </div>

        <div class="bg-[#1c1a18] p-6 rounded-lg border border-[#332f2b] space-y-3">
          <span class="text-xs font-mono uppercase tracking-widest text-[#d6b278]">03. Partisan Bias</span>
          <h3 class="text-xl font-medium text-white">Selective Acceptance</h3>
          <p class="text-base text-[#B3ADB9]">
            They habitually rejected absolute truth if it was voiced by rival groups or people they disliked.
          </p>
        </div>

        <div class="bg-[#1c1a18] p-6 rounded-lg border border-[#332f2b] space-y-3">
          <span class="text-xs font-mono uppercase tracking-widest text-[#d6b278]">04. Flawed Rationalization</span>
          <h3 class="text-xl font-medium text-white">Invalid Analogies</h3>
          <p class="text-base text-[#B3ADB9]">
            They constructed false intellectual equivalencies and unsound analogies to bypass clear divine commands.
          </p>
        </div>

      </div>

      <p class="text-lg sm:text-xl text-[#ECE8EF]">
        Intellectual confusion directly produced social dissolution. They remained perpetually divided and split into warring factions, while simultaneously viewing opposition to legitimate leadership and order as a badge of noble independence.
      </p>
    </section>

    <!-- PRINCIPLE III -->
    <section class="space-y-6 reveal-on-scroll">
      <div class="space-y-2 border-b border-[#332f2b] pb-4">
        <span class="text-sm font-mono uppercase tracking-widest text-[#d6b278]">Principle III</span>
        <h2 class="text-3xl font-medium text-white sm:text-4xl">
          Distortion of Revelation & Vain Delusions
        </h2>
      </div>

      <p class="text-lg sm:text-xl text-[#ECE8EF]">
        When confronted with scripture, those in state of <e>Jāhilīyyah</e> treated divine guidance with casual disrespect or deliberate malice. They refused to let revelation govern their lives, opting instead to bend scripture to suit their desires.
      </p>

      <div class="bg-[#24211e] p-6 sm:p-8 rounded-lg border border-[#332f2b] space-y-4">
        <ul class="space-y-4 text-base sm:text-lg text-[#ECE8EF]">
          <li class="flex items-start gap-3">
            <span class="text-[#d6b278] font-mono mt-1">•</span>
            <span><strong>Scriptural Tampering:</strong> They knowingly altered, distorted, and reinterpreted the Book of Allah to maintain status or evade moral duty.</span>
          </li>
          <li class="flex items-start gap-3">
            <span class="text-[#d6b278] font-mono mt-1">•</span>
            <span><strong>Levity in Religion:</strong> They treated sacred principles and acts of devotion as mere games, entertainment, and frivolous pastimes.</span>
          </li>
          <li class="flex items-start gap-3">
            <span class="text-[#d6b278] font-mono mt-1">•</span>
            <span><strong>Vain Wishful Thinking:</strong> They harbored false hopes of divine favor and salvation without putting forth righteous deeds or sincere repentance.</span>
          </li>
        </ul>
      </div>
    </section>

    <!-- PRINCIPLE IV -->
    <section class="space-y-8 reveal-on-scroll">
      <div class="space-y-2 border-b border-[#332f2b] pb-4">
        <span class="text-sm font-mono uppercase tracking-widest text-[#d6b278]">Principle IV</span>
        <h2 class="text-3xl font-medium text-white sm:text-4xl">
          Classism, Ancestral Pride, and Social Arrogance
        </h2>
      </div>

      <p class="text-lg sm:text-xl text-[#ECE8EF]">
        A defining hallmark of pre-Islamic ignorance was evaluating human worth through arbitrary worldly metrics—specifically lineage, status, and wealth.
      </p>

      <!-- Custom Numbered List -->
      <div class="bg-[#24211e] p-6 sm:p-8 rounded-lg border border-[#24211e] space-y-6">

        <div class="flex items-start gap-4">
          <span class="text-2xl sm:text-3xl font-bold font-mono text-[#d6b278]">01.</span>
          <div class="space-y-1">
            <h3 class="text-lg font-medium text-white sm:text-xl">Slandering Lineage</h3>
            <p class="text-base text-[#B3ADB9]">
              They used ancestral heritage as a weapon, pridefully exalting their own lineages while belittling the origins of others.
            </p>
          </div>
        </div>

        <div class="flex items-start gap-4 border-t border-[#332f2b] pt-6">
          <span class="text-2xl sm:text-3xl font-bold font-mono text-[#d6b278]">02.</span>
          <div class="space-y-1">
            <h3 class="text-lg font-medium text-white sm:text-xl">Scorn for the Poor</h3>
            <p class="text-base text-[#B3ADB9]">
              They actively looked down upon the impoverished and vulnerable, viewing economic hardship as a sign of spiritual or human inferiority.
            </p>
          </div>
        </div>

        <div class="flex items-start gap-4 border-t border-[#332f2b] pt-6">
          <span class="text-2xl sm:text-3xl font-bold font-mono text-[#d6b278]">03.</span>
          <div class="space-y-1">
            <h3 class="text-lg font-medium text-white sm:text-xl">Rejection of Humble Followers</h3>
            <p class="text-base text-[#B3ADB9]">
              They refused to accept the truth simply because those who initially embraced it were among the meek and socially disadvantaged.
            </p>
          </div>
        </div>

      </div>

      <!-- Conclusion Summary Card -->
      <div class="bg-[#24211e] border-l-2 border-[#d6b278] p-6 sm:p-8 rounded-r-lg space-y-3">
        <h3 class="text-xl font-medium text-white">Synthesis</h3>
        <p class="text-base sm:text-lg text-[#B3ADB9]">
          The traits of <e>Jāhilīyyah</e> are not confined to an ancient era; they are recurring human tendencies that resurface whenever divine revelation is set aside. By understanding these traits—grave veneration, reliance on majority opinion, classism, scriptural distortion, and social rebellion—the believer remains vigilant, safeguarding pure faith against ancient corruptions in modern forms.
        </p>
      </div>
    </section>
